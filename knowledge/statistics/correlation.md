# Correlation and causation

Correlation describes the strength and direction of an observed relationship between variables. Pearson correlation measures linear association; Spearman correlation measures monotonic rank association and is less sensitive to non-normal distributions. Neither establishes causation.

Check sample size, outliers, missingness, nonlinearity, and whether the relationship is stable across time or groups. A third variable may cause both observed variables to move together. Report the statistic, sample context, and uncertainty rather than describing a correlation as a cause.
